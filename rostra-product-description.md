# Rostra: Product Description

*The app as it stands on 6 November 2026. Status: pilot version for schools.*

## What Rostra is

Rostra is an AI coach for speaking. It does three things:

1. **Prepares you for a speech.** A presentation, a business pitch, a toast or any other occasion. The speech stays yours: Rostra coaches it and never writes it for you.
2. **Rehearses you for an oral exam.** Students practice with AI examiners who question them, follow up and grade them before the real exam.
3. **Makes you better over time.** The more you practice, study and use the app, the better you become, and the app shows that progress.

These parts work as one training system. No single feature is the whole product.

The name comes from the Rostra, the speakers' platform in the Roman Forum, where Cicero spoke.

## Why it exists

Rostra began with three needs of its creator:

- **Speeches that aren't generic.** The creator pitches and presents often, at business events, at university and elsewhere, and wanted a tool to prepare with. Generic speeches weren't good enough: each one had to carry the creator's own material and be tailored to the creator.
- **Better speaking, better thinking.** In the creator's words: "If I speak better, I think better." Articulating ideas well is a skill worth training for its own sake.
- **Readiness for oral exams.** Oral exams are returning to universities, and the creator expects them to spread. The creator wants to be ready for them and to be evaluated thoroughly with Rostra itself.

Two wider problems sit behind these needs:

- **Speaking is rarely trained.** It matters throughout school and work, yet few people practice it deliberately.
- **Honest feedback is hard to get.** A good coach or examiner often isn't available when needed, so most people rehearse alone.

## Who it serves

Students come first. They use both main parts of Rostra: oral exam rehearsal and speech preparation for presentations during their studies.

| Group | Their situation | What they get from Rostra |
|---|---|---|
| **Students** (lead group) | Oral exams from high school to PhD qualifying level, defending their own written work, and presentations | Graded exam rehearsal, flashcards and retakes aimed at their gaps, and help preparing presentations |
| **Professionals who present** | Pitches, presentations and team updates | Help building each talk, and feedback on recordings and meeting transcripts |
| **People with one big speech** | A wedding toast, a eulogy or a public speech that has to go well | A guided path from a blank page to a rehearsed speech in their own words |
| **Long-term skill builders** | Debaters, Toastmasters members and anyone training speaking as an ongoing practice | Daily drills, lessons, annotated great speeches and a visible record of progress |

**Possible use by universities.** Universities could also use Rostra as software to evaluate students. Today's app is built for individuals: each person sets up their own exams and sees their own results.

## What people walk away with

- **Readiness for the moment.** They pass the exam or deliver the speech that's coming up.
- **Real understanding.** They can explain and defend what they know, in addition to recalling it.
- **Confidence under pressure.** They stay composed when they're put on the spot.
- **A lasting skill.** They keep improving and aim higher through regular practice.

## The method

Rostra is built on Aristotle's three appeals, which it uses as a shared language across the whole app.

| Appeal | What it covers | Sub-skills |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethos** | Credibility and character | Credibility, goodwill, composure |
| **Pathos** | Emotion and imagination | Storytelling, vivid language, connection |
| **Logos** | Reason and evidence | Structure, evidence, reasoning |

Each analyzed speech is scored from 0 to 100 on all three appeals. The scores build a personal profile, shown as a triangle, and give the speaker a style such as The Storyteller, The Analyst or The Orator. A journey view plots how that style has moved, speech by speech.

Delivery numbers are measured on the user's device. AI writes the content feedback, exam questions, follow-ups, grades and coaching. When AI isn't available, the app falls back on word-pattern checks, a template question bank and self-marking.

## What the app does

### Prepare a speech

**Prepare** takes the user from a brief to a rehearsal in four stages:

1. **Brief.** The occasion, audience, goal and length, plus the user's own material and stories.
2. **Angles.** Three different approaches, each with a thesis, a sequence of beats, a mix of the three appeals, why it suits this speaker and its main risk. Three questions help the user find their best material.
3. **Build.** An outline of sections, each with a purpose, a named technique and a sentence frame with blanks to fill. The user writes every section, and the coach reviews it: what to keep, the most valuable change and any real problems, such as a fallacy or a cliché. When the user gets stuck, the coach offers questions and short opening fragments.
4. **Finish.** The full draft with a time estimate, teleprompter rehearsal, a slide outline, a download and a one-click route to feedback in Analyze.

Tailoring is the point of this module. Rostra shapes its approaches and sentence frames around the user's profile, their answers and an excerpt from an earlier speech, so that frames fit their natural voice. It leans on their strengths and gives them a safe way to stretch their weakest appeal.

### Get feedback on a speech

**Analyze** takes a recording (audio or video), a transcript, or both, along with the occasion and what the user wants feedback on. Transcripts can be pasted or uploaded, including meeting transcript files from Zoom, Teams or Meet. New users can open a sample report.

The report covers:

- **Scores.** An overall score, the three appeals, nine sub-skills and the speaker's style.
- **Delivery.** Pace, deliberate pauses versus frozen silences, pitch range, volume range and filler words, measured on the device. Rostra does not judge feelings from voice or face.
- **Content.** A map of the speech's structure and an annotated transcript that highlights strengths, rhetorical devices, fallacies, contradictions, vague claims and weak wording, with suggested fixes.
- **Coaching.** A note from the coach, three strengths, three next moves and three lessons written for the user's weak spots, each with a drill the coach grades.

Reports can be downloaded.

### Rehearse an oral exam

**Oral exam** recreates the pressure of a live examination. The student sets it up:

| Setting | Options |
|---|---|
| **Level** | High school, undergraduate, graduate, qualifying exam |
| **Format** | Quick (3 questions), Standard (5), Qualifying (7, before a panel of three), Why ladder (one claim, with follow-ups until understanding gives way) |
| **Examiner** | The Socratic, The Rigorous, The Encouraging, or The Panel: a Theorist, a Practitioner and a Skeptic |
| **Field** | Mixed, quantitative, interpretive, applied |
| **Basis** | A course or topic, optionally grounded in a pasted syllabus or course material, or the student's own written work |

**In the exam.** The examiner asks each question aloud, then a clock times both thinking and answering. The student can speak aloud and then note the points they made, speak into their device's dictation, or type. Examiners follow up by probing, challenging, asking for an example or extending the question.

**The result.** A grade (Distinction from 85%, Merit from 70%, Pass from 55%, otherwise Not yet) based on a rubric of accuracy (30%), depth (30%), reasoning (25%) and communication (15%). The report lists strengths, gaps with model-answer outlines, a question-by-question review, advice for the real exam and flashcards. When students defend their own work, it also says whether an examiner would believe they wrote it.

**The next attempt.** A retake starts from the gaps found last time and compares the two results. The app also keeps a list of weak concepts from earlier exams, and the student can start a short exam on any of them.

### Train between the big moments

- **Gym.** Five short stations of 1 to 3 minutes: Triangle Breathing to steady nerves, Vocal Warm-up, Tongue Twisters, Impromptu Roulette and Device Lab, which turns a plain sentence into one that lands.
- **Learn.** Fifteen core lessons, five for each appeal, each taking 3 to 4 minutes and including a quiz and a drill. Personal lessons written from the user's own speeches sit alongside them. Two games sharpen the ear: Fallacy Hunt and Device Spotter.
- **Library.** Twelve great speeches annotated move by move, including Pericles, Socrates, Elizabeth I, Abraham Lincoln, Frederick Douglass, Sojourner Truth, Emmeline Pankhurst and John F. Kennedy.
- **Coach chat.** Available from every page. The coach knows the user's level, profile and analyzed speeches.

### Keep people coming back

- **Home.** The user's profile, today's quests, their streak and what to do next.
- **Daily habit.** Three daily quests across different parts of the app, a laurel chest for finishing all three, and a daily streak.
- **Long-term progress.** Experience points, eleven named levels from Listener to Aristotle, and 22 badges.
- **Poster.** A generated poster of the user's rhetorical fingerprint that they can save.

## How the parts fit together

Rostra runs on a repeating loop:

1. **Perform.** Prepare a speech, deliver it or sit an exam.
2. **Get measured.** Scores, delivery numbers, grades and named gaps.
3. **Work on the weak spots.** Personal lessons, drills, flashcards and retakes.
4. **Try again and see the change.** The triangle, the journey view, retake comparisons, levels and badges.

The modules feed each other:

- A draft from Prepare goes to Analyze in one click.
- Each analysis updates the user's profile, which then shapes what Prepare suggests.
- Each exam's gaps become flashcards, weak concepts and the starting point for a retake.
- Daily quests lead users into parts of the app they might otherwise skip.
